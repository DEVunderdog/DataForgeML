# Measured bounds leave one floor bound and no Estimator Bounds

ADR-0091 gave the Feasibility Floor four bounds, and ADR-0094 gave the Estimator Ladder four more. None of them had a value. This map's rule is that thresholds arrive measured. So we measured all eight, against a scalar fill for the floor and against the rung below for the ladder. Six measured trivial or harmful and are deleted. The booster leaves the ladder. What survives is one bound: MICE needs at least 2 Rows per Predictor.

## Status

accepted — resolves [Measure the floor bounds and estimator bounds](https://github.com/DEVunderdog/DataForgeML/issues/543) on map #528, split out of #519. Amends ADR-0091 (three of its four bounds are deleted, and KNN has no floor, only its Resource Ceiling) and ADR-0094 (all four Estimator Bounds are deleted, the booster leaves the Estimator Ladder, and there is no downgrade). ADR-0094's "`max_iter` and `tol` read no Feasibility Term" is checked and stands.

## How it was measured

- **Failure means no better than the comparison.** Masked-cell RMSE ratio, median over 30 seeds with a 95% bootstrap CI. A bound is the smallest Usable Rows where the median ratio < 1 and its upper CI < 1. The floor compares against the fill routing would otherwise give (mean when |skew| ≤ 0.5, else median). An Estimator Bound compares against the rung below.
- **A 2D grid, not two 1D sweeps.** Rows per Predictor is Usable Rows ÷ predictors, so the two can't be swept apart. Usable Rows ∈ {10 … 2,500} × predictors ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10, 20} (KNN also 40). Whichever term the other one already explains measures trivial.
- **Synthetic:** one target at 20% MCAR with complete predictors. Linear, monotone, complex and latent/bimodal structures at population R² 0.3, 0.6 and 0.9, plus pure noise as a control. The signal sits in `min(p, 3)` predictors and the rest are noise, the worst case for Rows per Predictor. Bounds are read at R² 0.3, the plausible bottom of the middle Signal Tier. An Estimator Bound is read only on the structure whose tag gives that rung (forest on monotone, booster on complex), and only where that rung wins at 2,500 rows, meaning the tag's premise holds.
- **Real:** `ames` and `life_expectancy`. Targets with full-size R² ≥ 0.3 (8 per frame). Rows subsampled to the grid, and 20% of observed target cells held out. A frame's bound is the median across its targets. Real frames reach 1,000 Usable Rows, not 2,500, because a 500-row test set comes out of about 2,900 rows. `titanic` has no eligible target (Age reaches R² 0.20). Where synthetic and real disagree, real wins.
- **Machine:** at most 2,500 rows, one process, `ulimit -v` 2.5 GB. The scripts were throwaway and are not stored.

## Decisions

- **`mice_min_rows_per_predictor = 2`. `mice_min_usable_rows` is deleted.** On synthetic linear and latent data, BayesianRidge beats the scalar fill at 10 Usable Rows (the grid floor). As predictors grow, the threshold climbs to 20–30, which is Rows per Predictor ≈ 1.5–2. On `life_expectancy` (18 predictors), BayesianRidge overfits below Rows per Predictor ≈ 1. Schooling is 2.71× worse than the scalar fill at 10 rows, and the frame median threshold is 30 (≈ 1.7). `ames` passes at 10 on most targets. Given up: below 10 Usable Rows is unmeasured, so a one-predictor column with 3 usable rows passes by default, not because it is safe.
- **KNN has no Feasibility Floor.** `knn_min_usable_rows` and `knn_min_rows_per_predictor` are deleted. On real frames, KNN beats the scalar fill at 10 Usable Rows on 15 of 16 targets. The 16th, Population, never passes at any size, which is a signal problem, not a size problem. Synthetic data disagreed: at R² 0.3 with concentrated signal and pure-noise predictors, the threshold ran from 150 to above 2,500 rows as predictors grew. That is #542's dilution curve seen as a floor. Real predictors are correlated, not noise, so the real result stands. KNN keeps its Resource Ceiling (`knn_max_rows`). A zero-predictor column still gets a scalar, because its Signal Score is absent and routes as the bottom tier (ADR-0092). Given up: the synthetic dilution case (weak nonlinear signal, many unrelated predictors) has no size guard. It is handed to the Signal Score measurement.
- **ADR-0094's donor guarantee is withdrawn, not replaced.** `n_neighbors` lost its `n_rows - 1` cap partly because `knn_min_usable_rows` "guarantees donors". `KNNImputer` already uses every donor when there are fewer than `k`, so no guarantee is needed.
- **The forest carries no Estimator Bound.** `random_forest_min_usable_rows` and `random_forest_min_rows_per_predictor` are deleted. ADR-0094 assumed the forest is hungrier than BayesianRidge. The real frames refute that. The forest wins by the most at the *smallest* sizes, where BayesianRidge overfits: Schooling 0.25×, Life expectancy 0.43×, Hepatitis B 0.49× at 10 rows. At mid sizes some targets tie (CI touches 1), and the forest is never significantly worse. By the protocol's rule (the smallest passing size), the bound is 10: trivial. Given up: synthetic monotone data at R² 0.3 wanted 100–300 rows before the forest beats BayesianRidge. That case never showed as a loss on a real frame.
- **The booster leaves the Estimator Ladder.** `gradient_boost_min_usable_rows` and `gradient_boost_min_rows_per_predictor` are deleted, and `ComplexNonlinear` maps to the forest. On real frames the booster loses to the forest by 1–9% at every size up to 1,000 on 10 of 12 targets. Only SalePrice and Year Built pass, from 300–500. Its only possible winning regime is above 1,000 real rows, which this box can't measure: MICE with a forest hit OOM at 1,500 Usable Rows under the cap. The protocol forbids extrapolating a bound, and a rung with an unmeasured default breaks this map's rule. `GradientBoostingRegressor` stays reachable through `with_model_choice`. This is the "delete the booster branch" option ADR-0094 rejected on memory evidence, now taken on accuracy evidence. Given up: on a large-memory machine with more than 1,000 usable rows, a `ComplexNonlinear` block loses the booster's synthetic 5–9% gain unless the user sets it.
- **The Estimator Ladder reads no Feasibility Term.** With no Estimator Bounds left, there is no downgrade, no thinnest-column minimum, and no "downgraded from" signal. The block's most complex tag picks the estimator: `Linear` and `Unpredictable` → BayesianRidge, `MonotonicNonlinear` and `ComplexNonlinear` → forest. ADR-0094's "downgrade, never reject" reasoning has nothing left to govern. Given up: nothing measured. A thin column no longer changes its block's estimator.
- **`max_iter` and `tol` still read no Feasibility Term.** A 4-column block at 20% MCAR, `max_iter = 10`, default `tol`. BayesianRidge (30 seeds): linear data converges *faster* with more rows (6.2 → 3.4 iterations, Spearman ρ = −0.71), complex drifts slightly the other way (ρ = +0.13), and monotone is flat. That is no consistent direction, and the cap binds in only 2–3% of fits. Forest (10 seeds, Usable Rows ≤ 1,000): the cap binds in 95–100% of fits at every size, because a forest never meets `tol`. Size can't move a cap that always binds.

## Measured caveats

- **Usable Rows is not fully truthful for MICE.** At the same Usable Rows, a column that's heavily missing in a 2,500-row frame does worse than one at 20% missing in a small frame. On linear data the gap is 2–6% (0.958 vs 0.916 at 50 Usable Rows, 0.936 vs 0.883 at 300). Both still beat the scalar fill, so pass/fail never flips. On complex data under BayesianRidge, the heavy-missing arm is worse than the scalar fill (1.10–1.15) up to 300 Usable Rows. The likely cause is heavily mean-filled target values feeding back through the chained equations. KNN shows no such gap. No third term is added: on the structures BayesianRidge serves, the penalty never flips a decision. The complex-under-BayesianRidge case is reachable only through `Unpredictable → BayesianRidge`.
- **The forest never converges.** It hits `max_iter` in 95–100% of fits at every measured size. That is a fact about `tol` under a stochastic estimator, not about size, and this ticket does not settle it.

## Considered Options

- **Two 1D sweeps.** Cheaper, but they can't show which term does the work, and that is the deletion test.
- **Keep the KNN Rows per Predictor bound at the synthetic value.** It would refuse KNN on real frames where KNN beats the scalar fill at 10 rows.
- **Keep the booster with its bounds "above 1,000, unmeasured"** and hand choosing a default to implementation or a bigger machine. Keeps a real choice for large-memory users, but ships a dial nobody measured, on a rung that loses on every measured real target but two.
- **A third floor term for the missing fraction.** It would capture the Q2 penalty, but it never flips a decision on a served structure, and it would add an unmeasured dial.

## Consequences

- Configuration loses seven dials: `mice_min_usable_rows`, `knn_min_usable_rows`, `knn_min_rows_per_predictor`, `random_forest_min_usable_rows`, `random_forest_min_rows_per_predictor`, `gradient_boost_min_usable_rows`, `gradient_boost_min_rows_per_predictor`. It keeps `mice_min_rows_per_predictor = 2` and `knn_max_rows`.
- ADR-0081's "the budget goes dead for `ComplexNonlinear` frames above 10 000 rows" no longer happens by default. That block now takes the forest, which has an `n_jobs`. It still happens when a user sets the booster.
- Handed to [Measure the signal weights, tier boundaries and R² discount](https://github.com/DEVunderdog/DataForgeML/issues/544): the KNN dilution case, the only failure on a synthetic frame with no size guard left, and the `Unpredictable → BayesianRidge` heavy-missingness loss.
